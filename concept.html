Flower Lab

Concept

One pot · one cabinet

A tabletop cabinet for one plant.

You set the light, how moist the soil should stay, and how strong the feed should be. The cabinet keeps the day, gives measured drinks, and mixes the recipe before it reaches the pot.

  • About 40 cm wide
  • About 70 cm tall
  • Raspberry Pi, in the room

Goals

What you can count on.

Light

A day, kept.

You set hours and brightness. The hood keeps that schedule. A sensor at the leaves confirms the plant was actually lit.

If the bay gets too warm, the light backs off.

Working means a day in the log matches the schedule, and the sensor saw light while the hood was on.

Water

A measured drink.

You set how moist the soil should stay. The cabinet gives a small drink, waits, and checks again.

It stops if the tank is low, the soil is already wet, or the day’s cap is hit.

Working means every drink has a volume and a reason it stopped.

Feed

A recipe, then the pot.

You set how strong the feed should be. On a feeding, water is mixed with A and B in a cup until the mix measures right. It checks that the mix is not too acidic or too alkaline, then sends it up to the drip ring.

If it cannot hit the target, it holds and tells you. A and B never pour straight into the soil.

Working means a feeding either lands in the band or is held. Never an unmeasured pour.

Feed

Mixed before it leaves.

Three bottles live in the base: water, concentrate A, and concentrate B. Small pumps move them into a cup. Nothing goes to the plant from the bottles themselves.

Two probes in the cup read strength and acidity. The cabinet adds a little more water, A, or B until the mix sits where you asked. Only then does the tube carry it up to the drip ring.

The cabinet does not read nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium out of the soil. It controls the recipe it mixes, then delivers that recipe. That is the part you can trust.

Limits

Not this.

  • Not a row of pots. One plant, one saucer, one set of bottles. A shelf of pots is a later cabinet.
  • Not a cloud account. The page you use lives on the Raspberry Pi. Schedules, history, and alerts stay in the room.
  • Not a soil lab. No probe that pretends to name the nutrients in the pot. The measurement that matters is the mix in the cup.
